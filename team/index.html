What she does

What makes her an employee, not a tool.

She works in your conversations

Talk to her in a thread with your team. Say “get this approved by Thursday” and the task exists — you don’t fill in a form.

She finishes things

Reconciling a statement, assembling a deck, collecting documents. She works through the steps and comes back with the result.

She chases people

Your team, your accountant, a supplier who isn’t a Kntara user. She emails, reminds, and reports who still hasn’t replied.

She knows your business

Give her your policies and files once. She answers from them — the travel policy, the approval limits, last quarter’s numbers.

Her own dashboard

Every employee gets a page, and you decide what is on it.

Each virtual employee has her own dashboard. Choose what she keeps in front of you — what she is working on right now, what she is waiting on somebody else for, and the handful of numbers that matter for her job. Arrange it once and it stays that way.

  • Different per employee. Your CFO’s page and your recruiter’s page have nothing in common, and neither should.
  • Built from her actual work. Everything on it is something she did or is waiting on — not a report you have to ask for.
  • Rearrange it whenever. Add what you find yourself asking her for, drop what you stopped reading.
People outside your company

Most of the work isn’t finished inside your company.

The supplier, the accountant, the candidate, the client who owes you a signature — she deals with all of them directly, and none of them ever hears the word Kntara or signs up for anything.

This is the part that usually falls back on you. It doesn’t here.

  • No account, ever. She emails them a private link to the one task they are part of. They reply, upload a file or approve something in the browser and that is the whole experience.
  • They see the task, not the thread. Nothing else from your organisation is visible to them — not the conversation it came from, not who else is on it.
  • She chases on their schedule. Reminders go out on their working hours, not hers, and she keeps going until they answer or you tell her to stop.
  • You see who is holding things up. Every reply, file and approval lands back in the task, with who sent it and when — and who still hasn’t.

How it works

Three steps, then she’s working.

Setting her up takes about the time you’d spend writing a job description — because that’s roughly what you’re doing.

  • Hire her. Pick the role she fills and how experienced she needs to be. Give her a name.
  • Brief her. Set the rules she follows and upload anything she should know. Change them whenever you like.
  • Put her to work. Add her to a conversation and start handing things over. She tells you when she needs a decision.

Two things are never limited by her hours

She always receives. Messages, files and approvals land the moment they’re sent, whatever the hour — she just works on them during her shift.

She always chases. Reminders to people outside your company go out on their schedule, not hers. Nobody waits until tomorrow for a nudge.

Questions people ask

Before you hire her

Is she going to make things up?

She answers from what you’ve given her, and she tells you when she doesn’t know rather than guessing. When she uses one of your documents she says which one.

Who can see our information?

Your files and conversations belong to your organisation. People outside it only ever see the specific task you invited them to — never the thread it came from.

What if she gets something wrong?

Every task shows what she did and when. You can stop her mid-task, correct her, and she carries on. Anything with real consequences waits for your approval first.

Can we cancel?

Yes, whenever you like. Nothing is locked in and nothing you’ve paid for expires.
